A journey through time...

Story & History

From ancient fields to modern times, sugarcane has been part of human civilisations for thousands of years. It has sweetened celebrations, strengthened communities, and stood the test of time.

Originating in New Guinea, sugarcane spread across Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the world. It played a vital role in trade, economies, and cultures for centuries.

Planting

Sugarcane is not grown from seed. Short pieces of a mature stalk, called setts, are laid in rich, moist soil.

Each joint carries a sleeping bud that wakes, sends down roots and pushes up a fresh green shoot.

Uses of Sugarcane

One plant, endless goodness – nothing from the cane goes to waste.

  • Food & Beverages

    Fresh juice, jaggery, syrups and sweets.

  • Natural Sweetener

    Sugar, jaggery and molasses for the kitchen.

  • Traditional Remedies

    Long used in home remedies for energy and cooling.

  • Biofuel (Ethanol)

    Clean-burning fuel made from cane juice.

  • Animal Feed

    Leaves and tops feed livestock on the farm.

  • Paper & Packaging

    Bagasse fibre becomes plates, paper and boards.

Sugarcane in Our Culture

Sweetness that brings us together

A part of festivals, traditions, rituals and celebrations. It connects generations and brings people together.

At harvest festivals whole canes are tied at the door, shared with neighbours and chewed by children in the sun. At weddings and temple offerings it stands for a sweet, plentiful life ahead.

Every glass we press carries a little of that same feeling: something simple, fresh and shared.
